PLASTIC POLLUTION HEALTH EFFECTS

Plastic pollution causes extensive environmental and health damage:

Marine Environment Effects:
- Over 800 marine species affected by plastic ingestion
- Entanglement of marine mammals, sea turtles, and seabirds
- Ingestion of plastic by fish, birds, and marine animals causing starvation
- Microplastics entering the food chain at base levels
- Damage to coral reefs from plastic waste
- Transport of invasive species on floating plastics

Human Health Effects:
- Microplastics found in drinking water, food, and air
- Average person ingests 5 grams of plastic per week (credit card sized)
- Chemical leaching from plastics into food and water (BPA, phthalates)
- Endocrine disruption from plastic additives
- Potential carcinogenic effects from plastic chemicals
- Inhalation of microplastic fibers in indoor air

Terrestrial Effects:
- Soil contamination affecting plant growth
- Blockage of drainage systems causing flooding
- Visual pollution in natural landscapes
- Groundwater contamination from landfill leachate
- Harm to terrestrial wildlife that ingest or become entangled

Environmental Persistence:
- Plastic takes 400+ years to decompose
- Only 9% of all plastic ever produced has been recycled
- 12% has been incinerated
- 79% has accumulated in landfills or environment
- By 2050, there could be more plastic than fish in oceans

Economic Costs:
- $13 billion annual damage to marine ecosystems
- Cleanup costs for municipalities ($1-5 billion per year in coastal cities)
- Damage to tourism and recreation industries
- Loss of fisheries and aquaculture revenue
- Reduced property values in polluted areas

Sources of Plastic Pollution:
- Single-use plastics (bags, bottles, straws, packaging)
- Fishing gear (ghost nets)
- Microplastics from synthetic clothing
- Tire wear particles
- Plastic pellets from manufacturing
- Personal care products with microbeads
