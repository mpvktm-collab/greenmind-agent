OCEAN ACIDIFICATION EFFECTS

Ocean acidification threatens marine ecosystems and global food security:

Chemical Changes:
- Ocean pH decreased by 0.1 since industrial revolution (30% increase in acidity)
- Projected pH decrease of 0.3-0.4 by 2100 (150% increase in acidity)
- CO2 absorption rate: 2.5 billion tons annually
- Oceans have absorbed 30% of human CO2 emissions
- Carbonate ion concentration reduced by 16% since 1800

Marine Life Effects:
- Coral calcification rates reduced by 40% since pre-industrial times
- Oyster larvae survival reduced by 50% in acidified waters
- Clam and mussel shell growth reduced by 25-30%
- Sea urchin larval development impaired
- Pteropods (sea butterflies) shell dissolution occurring
- Crustaceans have difficulty forming exoskeletons

Coral Reef Collapse:
- Great Barrier Reef lost 50% of coral cover since 1985
- 90% of coral reefs could be lost by 2050
- Coral bleaching events now 5x more frequent
- Reefs provide habitat for 25% of marine species
- $375 billion annual economic value from coral reefs

Fisheries Impact:
- Shellfish aquaculture threatened ($1 billion annual US industry)
- Salmon fisheries affected by food web disruption
- Cod and haddock populations declining
- 3 billion people depend on seafood for protein
- Estimated $100 billion annual fishery losses by 2100

Food Web Disruption:
- Pteropods (base of Antarctic food web) shells dissolving
- Krill populations affected by pH changes
- Salmon, whales, and seals depend on krill and pteropods
- Plankton diversity and abundance decreasing
- Marine food web collapse threatens entire ocean ecosystem

Economic Consequences:
- Global fisheries economic loss: $50-100 billion annually by 2100
- Coral reef tourism loss: $10-20 billion annually
- Shellfish industry loss: $1-5 billion annually
- Coastal protection loss: $50 billion annually
- 100-200 million people could face food insecurity

Regional Hotspots:
- Arctic Ocean (most rapid acidification)
- Southern Ocean (pteropod critical habitat)
- Pacific Northwest US (shellfish industry crisis)
- Great Barrier Reef (coral collapse)
- Caribbean reefs (coral bleaching)
- Mediterranean Sea (warming and acidification combined)

Mitigation Requirements:
- Reduce global CO2 emissions by 50% by 2030
- Achieve net-zero by 2050
- Protect and restore seagrass and mangroves
- Reduce local pollution and nutrient runoff
- Establish marine protected areas
- Develop acidification monitoring networks
