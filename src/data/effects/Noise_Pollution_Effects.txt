NOISE POLLUTION EFFECTS

Noise pollution causes significant health and environmental damage:

Human Health Effects:
- Hearing loss and tinnitus
- Sleep disturbance and insomnia (affects 10% of Europeans)
- Cardiovascular disease including hypertension and heart attacks
- Stress, anxiety, and depression
- Cognitive impairment in children (reduced reading comprehension)
- Reduced work performance and productivity
- Increased risk of stroke

Wildlife Effects:
- Disruption of animal communication and mating calls
- Altered migration patterns
- Increased stress hormones in animals
- Avoidance of quality habitats
- Reduced reproductive success
- Changes in predator-prey relationships

Specific Wildlife Impacts:
- Birds singing at higher frequencies to overcome noise
- Marine mammals stranded due to sonar and ship noise
- Whales altering migration routes to avoid shipping lanes
- Reduced foraging efficiency in bats and owls
- Frogs and amphibians affected in breeding calls

Urban Environment:
- Reduced quality of life in cities
- Property value depreciation near noise sources (10-30% reduction)
- Conflicts between residential and commercial areas
- Increased costs for soundproofing
- Reduced property values

Sources of Noise Pollution:
- Road traffic (80% of environmental noise in cities)
- Aircraft noise near airports
- Railway noise along train lines
- Industrial noise from factories
- Construction noise
- Recreational noise (music venues, sporting events)

Noise Level Reference:
- 30 dB: Quiet library
- 60 dB: Normal conversation
- 85 dB: Heavy traffic (hearing damage with prolonged exposure)
- 100 dB: Jackhammer (hearing damage after 15 minutes)
- 120 dB: Rock concert (immediate hearing risk)
- 140 dB: Jet engine (pain threshold)

Vulnerable Populations:
- Children (cognitive development impacts)
- Elderly (cardiovascular risks)
- Shift workers (sleep disruption)
- People in low-income neighborhoods near noise sources
- Hospital patients (delayed healing)
- School children (learning impairment)
