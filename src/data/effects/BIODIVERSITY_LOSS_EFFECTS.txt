BIODIVERSITY LOSS EFFECTS

Biodiversity loss has cascading effects on ecosystems and human well-being:

Ecosystem Service Loss:
- Reduced pollination affecting crop production (estimated $235-577 billion annual loss)
- Decline in natural pest control (increased pesticide use)
- Loss of water purification capacity (higher treatment costs)
- Reduced carbon sequestration (accelerated climate change)
- Decreased soil formation and nutrient cycling
- Loss of flood and storm protection

Species Extinction Crisis:
- Current extinction rates 100-1000 times higher than natural background rate
- 1 million species threatened with extinction globally
- Loss of keystone species causing ecosystem collapse
- Genetic diversity loss reducing adaptation capacity
- Disruption of food webs and ecological relationships

Human Impacts:
- Loss of potential medicines (many drugs derived from nature)
- Reduced food security from fishery and crop diversity loss
- Increased vulnerability to natural disasters
- Loss of cultural and aesthetic values
- Reduced ecotourism revenue

Economic Costs:
- Ecosystem services valued at $125-145 trillion annually
- Billions lost in fisheries from overexploitation
- Increased costs for artificial pollination
- Higher water treatment expenses
- Loss of genetic resources for breeding programs

Climate Change Connection:
- Forests store 30% of annual CO2 emissions
- Mangroves and wetlands are critical carbon sinks
- Biodiversity loss reduces ecosystem resilience
- Degraded ecosystems release stored carbon

Hotspots:
- Amazon rainforest (10% of world's species)
- Coral reefs (25% of marine species)
- Southeast Asian rainforests
- African savannas
- Arctic and Antarctic ecosystems
