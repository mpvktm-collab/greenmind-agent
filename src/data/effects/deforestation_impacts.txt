DEFORESTATION IMPACTS ON CLIMATE CHANGE

Deforestation significantly accelerates climate change through multiple mechanisms:

Carbon Emissions:
- Forests store 45% of terrestrial carbon
- Deforestation releases 4-5 billion tons of CO2 annually (10-15% of global emissions)
- Clearing one hectare of forest releases 200-500 tons of CO2

Loss of Carbon Sinks:
- Forests absorb 2.5 billion tons of CO2 each year
- Deforestation removes this critical carbon sink
- Tropical deforestation reduces carbon sequestration by 30-40%

Albedo Effect Changes:
- Forests have lower albedo than cleared land
- Deforestation can cause local temperature increases of 2-5°C
- Reduced evapotranspiration leads to less cloud formation

Rainfall Disruption:
- Forests generate 50-80% of their own rainfall
- Deforestation reduces rainfall by 20-40% in affected regions
- Amazon deforestation could reduce rainfall by 30-50%

Feedback Loops:
- Drier conditions increase fire risk
- Fires release more carbon and kill remaining trees
- Degraded forests emit more carbon than they absorb

Regional Impacts:
- Southeast Asia: Deforestation contributes 1.5 billion tons CO2/year
- Amazon: Deforestation emissions equivalent to 5% of global total
- Congo Basin: Deforestation threatens world's second largest rainforest

Global Impact:
- Deforestation accounts for 10-15% of total greenhouse gas emissions
- Ending deforestation could reduce global emissions by 30% by 2050
- Reforestation could sequester 1-2 billion tons CO2 annually
