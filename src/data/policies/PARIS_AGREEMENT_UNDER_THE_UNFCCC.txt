PARIS AGREEMENT UNDER THE UNFCCC (2015)

The Paris Agreement is a legally binding international treaty on climate change. It was adopted by 196 Parties at COP 21 in Paris on 12 December 2015 and entered into force on 4 November 2016.

Key Goals (Article 2):
- Hold the increase in the global average temperature to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels.
- Pursue efforts to limit the temperature increase to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels.
- Increase the ability to adapt to the adverse impacts of climate change and foster climate resilience.
- Make finance flows consistent with a pathway towards low greenhouse gas emissions and climate-resilient development.

Key Provisions:
- Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs): Each Party must prepare, communicate and maintain successive NDCs, representing a progression over time (Article 4).
- Global Stocktake: A periodic review of collective progress towards the Agreement's goals, first conducted in 2023 and repeated every five years (Article 14).
- Adaptation: Establishes a global goal on adaptation of enhancing adaptive capacity, strengthening resilience and reducing vulnerability (Article 7).
- Loss and Damage: Recognizes the importance of averting, minimizing and addressing loss and damage associated with climate change impacts (Article 8).
- Finance: Developed country Parties shall provide financial resources to assist developing country Parties (Article 9).
- Transparency: An enhanced transparency framework for action and support is established (Article 13).
